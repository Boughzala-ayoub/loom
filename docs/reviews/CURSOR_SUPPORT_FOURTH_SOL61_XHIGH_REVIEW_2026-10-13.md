## 1. A stop written before an Enter can finish that Enter's receipt

`agentReport` (`src/main.ts:5094`) calls `p.wake(undefined, "hook")` when the current turn stops. `Pane.wake` (2692) reads the pane's current `lastIn` and `Date.now()` and counts a finished turn, and `turnReceipts` (4329) and `finishReceipt` then settle any receipt waiting on that pane.

A starts at 1000 and Loom has heard its start. Cursor ends A at 2000, but the stop file waits for the next poll. B's Enter goes in at 2050 and its start is stamped 2100. The poll at 2200 handles A's stop first. `cursorGen` is still `gA`, so `agentReport` calls `wake`, which counts a turn and finishes B's receipt. B's start then makes B working, as it should. The result is A completed or failed, B working, the pane busy and not waiting, and B's `receipt.result.turn` at 1. B's receipt has `acceptedAt` 2050 and `steps.entering` and `steps.submitted` 2050, so this isn't missing timing data. `waitReceipt` (8764) returns `done: true` as soon as `r.result` exists and doesn't look at the task, so automation can move on while B still works. The case fails for A's completion and for A's failure.

Handling an old report now doesn't mean a turn stopped after B went in. The fix should compare the stop report's timestamp with the receipt's Enter time, as well as the turn's identity. B's own later stop must still settle its receipt, and A's task must still end with its notice. Every caller of `wake` and of the receipts must keep working, other agents and shell receipts included.

## 2. An Esc can hide a failure written before it

`agentReport` turns a fail into an interrupt (5085) when the turn is in `cursorEsc`. `Pane.wrote` (around 2990) adds the current turn on an Esc, and a start report picks up a pending Esc stamped after it (5076).

A starts at 1000. A genuinely fails and writes its error at 2000, which Loom hasn't polled. B's Enter goes in at 2050, B starts at 2100, and an Esc interrupts B at 2150. `wrote` still sees A as the running turn and adds `gA` to `cursorEsc`. At the 2200 poll A's earlier failure becomes an interrupt, and B's start takes the pending Esc, so B cancels too. The result is [A cancelled, B cancelled, no notices], where [A failed, B cancelled, one failed notice] is expected. A failure written before an Esc can't have been caused by that Esc.

The same loss happens when Loom has polled neither start. A's Enter goes in at 2000, its start is stamped 2020 and its genuine failure 2060. B's Enter goes in at 2090 and its start is stamped 2110. An Esc goes in at 2150, and B's error and aborted are stamped 2170 and 2180. All of these reports arrive at 2200. The start binding adds `gA` because A's start precedes the latest Esc, though A's failure precedes it too. A should fail and B cancel, but both cancel and no failure notice goes out.

The fix should keep the interruption times each turn and each pending Esc needs, and compare them with the stop's timestamp, so a failure stamped before its interruption stays a failure. Early cancellations and several cancellations must still work. A check against only the latest `cursorEscAt` would break an older turn that really was interrupted before that Esc.

The protection case passes on `7042dbc` and must keep passing. A's Enter goes in at 2000 and its start is stamped 2020. An Esc interrupts A at 2050, and A's error and aborted are stamped 2070 and 2080. B's Enter goes in at 2090 and its start is stamped 2110. An Esc interrupts B at 2150, and B's error and aborted are stamped 2170 and 2180. All arrive in one poll at 2200. Both turns must end cancelled with no failure notice, though A's interruption precedes the latest Esc. Both stop orders and interleaved stops must keep working too.
